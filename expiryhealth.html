Expired stock is a quiet, expensive problem

Every month, health systems use staff to physically check expiration dates, shelf by shelf, fridge by fridge, room by room, and still miss stock until it's wasted or, worse, used. Expiry tracking itself isn't new, people have checked dates by hand and on spreadsheets for years. ExpiryHealth makes it faster, cheaper, and easier: it helps you rotate stock so you use what expires first, waste less, and make sure nothing expired ever reaches a patient. The honest threshold: a spreadsheet can run expiry for one practice with one or two people ordering. ExpiryHealth is built for exactly that moment, and priced to beat the spreadsheet's only advantage: free. Why it pays

Save the hours. Save the stock. Keep patients safe.

ExpiryHealth isn't just a list of dates. It's built around four outcomes every health system and clinic already cares about, time, money, compliance, and safety.

1  Save staff hours every month

Right now, staff check cabinets, fridges and exam rooms physically reading every expiry date, every month, skilled clinical time spent hunting dates instead of caring for patients. ExpiryHealth does that check for you. The system watches every lot continuously and hands your team a short, location-by-location list of only what needs attention. The monthly sweep drops from hours of checking to about 15 minutes of pulling and clearing.

2  Save money with the 6-month macro lens

Most waste happens because short-dated stock sits at the back of the shelf until it's too late. ExpiryHealth flips the view: look six months ahead, across every location at once, and you can see what's going to expire in time to act. Rotate that stock forward and use it first, or use it before you order more. Stock you already paid for gets used, not written off and thrown away.

3  Be ready for your survey, whoever runs it

Whoever oversees your facility, The Joint Commission, AAHA for accredited vet practices, a state board, the FDA, inspectors don't just check that expired items are gone; they check that you have a reliable, repeatable process for finding them. And if your facility answers to no surveyor at all, that same documented process is simply good practice. ExpiryHealth gives every facility that process: scheduled checks on a set cadence, per-facility warning windows, a flag for anything expiring this month, keep a close eye on it or remove it immediately, and an extreme alert on the last business day of the month. When an inspector asks “who checked what, and when,” you have the history to show them.

4  Make sure no one is harmed

This is the reason the other three matter. An expired item that stays on the shelf can be used on a patient. ExpiryHealth covers every medical item in the inventory, medications, vaccines, implants, devices and supplies, tracked by lot and expiry, at every site. Flag it early, rotate it if it's still good, pull it the moment it's not. Nothing expired stays available to use.

Waste report: see what keeps expiring most frequently

Built into the app and generated from your live inventory: a waste report that lists every item removed from circulation in the previous month, ranked highest to lowest. Work down that list and lower the par levels for the items at the top, so the facility orders less of what it keeps throwing away, less waste, and more money saved. The report auto-generates in the first week of each month and is emailed to your administrator as a spreadsheet.

The product

How ExpiryHealth works

Every item, medication, vaccine, implant, device or supply, is tracked by name, lot, expiry date, quantity and site. Lots are tracked by expiry, deliberately not as a live stock-level system, so the one job stays fast and clear: see six months ahead, use what expires first, and pull anything expired before it can be used.

1

Add stock at receiving

Enter name, lot, expiry, quantity and site, or import a screenshot at receiving and check everything on a review screen before anything is saved. Location is entered manually unless it's already listed on your spreadsheet or inventory invoice, so you don't have to type it twice.

2

Set the 6-month macro lens

Every facility gets its own warning window, 30 to 180 days, in 30-day steps. At 180 days you see the full six months ahead, so you can rotate short-dated stock forward and use it first instead of finding it expired. Anything expiring this month is flagged, with two options, keep a close eye on it or remove it immediately, whatever the window says.

3

Checks on your schedule

Pick weekly, biweekly or monthly checks, with a day and time per facility (the default is Thursday, 10:00am). Need an answer now? Hit Check now any time for an on-demand sweep.

4

Alerts the way your team wants them

In-app alerts can be switched on or off. Email alerts are optional, can go to any address, and let you choose the frequency and which alert types are sent.

5

The last-business-day alarm

On the last business day of every month, ExpiryHealth raises an extreme alert for anything about to roll into “expiring this month”, the final chance to pull it before it expires.

6

Rotate, sweep, pull, done

Work two lists: the rotation list (expiring in the next six months, move it forward, use it first) and the expiring-this-month list by location (pull the stock, clear the flag, or keep a close eye on it). The next check starts clean, and the monthly sweep is a 15-minute routine instead of a cabinet-by-cabinet hunt.

Straight answers

Questions & open items

How does ExpiryHealth help with surveys and inspections?
Whatever oversight your facility works under, The Joint Commission, AAHA accreditation for vet practices, a state board, the FDA, or no formal survey at all, it turns expiry checking from an ad-hoc, person-dependent hunt into a documented, repeatable process: scheduled checks on a set cadence per facility, warning windows of 30–180 days, a flag for anything expiring this month, keep a close eye on it or remove it immediately, and an extreme alert on the last business day of the month. That creates the check history, who checked what, and when, that an inspector asks to see. ExpiryHealth supports your compliance process; it doesn't replace your own policies or guarantee a survey outcome.
Where is the waste report, in the app?
Yes, the waste report is built into the app. It auto-generates in the first week of each month for the previous month and is emailed to your administrator as a spreadsheet attachment, ranked highest to lowest, so a manager can lower par levels on the items at the top of the list and order less of what keeps being thrown away.
What does the 6-month macro lens actually do?
Instead of only seeing what's about to expire, you see every lot expiring in the next six months, across every site, in one view. That lead time is what saves money: stock with four months left can be rotated to the front, used first, or held off the next order, instead of being discovered expired at month six and written off.
What kinds of items does it cover?
Any medical item in the inventory with an expiry date, medications, vaccines, implants, devices and general supplies, tracked by name, lot, expiry, quantity and site. If it can expire and be used on a patient, it belongs in ExpiryHealth.
Does ExpiryHealth store patient information?
No. It tracks items, lots, expiry dates, quantities and locations only, no patient or customer records enter the system.
Does it track live stock levels?
No, lots are tracked by expiry, not by live on-hand counts. That is a deliberate scope choice to keep the sweep fast.
What does ExpiryHealth cost?
Two plans, locked in, priced per facility: Basic is $25/month per facility, one building, the per-facility warning clock, screenshot import capped at 10 screenshots or inventory lists, and in-app alerts. Plus is $45/month per facility, everything in Basic, plus unlimited screenshots and inventory lists, email alerts, audit report exports and more sites within the building. Inside one facility there is no per-user fee, and both plans start with a free trial.

Privacy Policy: ExpiryHealth

Effective date: 23 November 2026

Last updated: 23 November 2026

ExpiryHealth is an inventory management tool for clinics and small facilities. It helps your team track medical, veterinary and dental supplies by expiry date so nothing expired reaches a patient. It is not a medical device and does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

This policy explains what information we collect, why we collect it, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and how you can ask us to delete it. It applies to the ExpiryHealth website (expiryhealth.com) and the ExpiryHealth Android app (and, when it exists, the iPhone app). The app is published by Scree and Sage, LLC (“we”, “us”), a limited liability company registered in Idaho, USA.

If you have any question about this policy, email us at info@expiryhealth.com.

1. What we collect

We collect only what the app needs to work. We do not collect patient information, patient health records, or health data about any individual. ExpiryHealth tracks supplies, the items on your shelves, not people.

1.1 Account details (required to use the app)

When you create an account, we collect:

  • your name;
  • your email address;
  • your password (stored only as a secure hash by our authentication provider, we never see or store your plain-text password);
  • the facility (or facilities) your account belongs to, and your role in that facility.

We use this to let you sign in, to keep each facility's data separate from every other facility's, and to contact you about your account (for example, password-reset codes).

1.2 Inventory data (the data you enter, required)

Everything you put into the app to do your job:

  • item names, lot numbers, expiry dates, quantities and units;
  • the rooms/locations (and location groups) where each lot is stocked;
  • optional fields you choose to fill in, such as the NDC number and how many units were taken out of circulation before expiry;
  • your facility's settings (alert schedule, warning-clock preferences, administrator email address).

We use this to run the app for you: the warning clock, the monthly sweep, alerts, and the monthly waste report. Your inventory data is visible only to accounts in your own facility (and to us only as described in §3, for running the service). It is never sold, and never used for advertising.

1.3 Photos and screenshots (optional, you choose when to send them)

When you use screenshot import, you send us a photo of a shelf label or an inventory list so the app can read the expiry dates from it. You review everything the import reads before it is saved, and you can discard it.

  • Photos are uploaded to our server so the import can run. They are stored with your facility's inventory data.
  • Photos may incidentally show whatever was on the shelf or list when you photographed it. Do not photograph people, patients, or anything containing patient information, the import only needs the label.
  • You can use the app fully without ever sending a photo (enter items by hand instead).

1.4 Payment information (handled by Stripe, not by us)

Subscriptions are paid through Stripe. When you subscribe, Stripe collects your payment details (such as your card number) on its own secure pages. We never see, store, or touch your full card number. What we do receive and store from Stripe is: your subscription status (Basic $25/month or Plus $45/month per facility, trial status, renewal date), the last four digits and expiry of the card on file, and Stripe's customer/subscription identifiers so we can match a payment to your facility. Stripe's own privacy policy governs the payment details it collects: https://stripe.com/privacy.

1.5 Emails you receive, and how you use the app

  • Alert and report emails: the administrator email address you give us, and which alerts you have turned on, so we can send expiry alerts, password-reset codes, and the monthly waste report. Our email provider is listed in §3.
  • Support emails: anything you send to info@expiryhealth.com or help@expiryhealth.com, so we can answer you.
  • Basic service records: when you sign in, and error/crash records the app generates so we can fix bugs. These records contain technical details (such as app version and device type), not your inventory data.

1.6 What we do NOT collect

  • No patient names, dates of birth, medical records, diagnoses, or any other health information about individuals.
  • No precise location (GPS), “locations” in the app are room names you type in (for example, “Exam Room 1”), not where a device is.
  • No contacts, no microphone recordings, no advertising identifier, and no data for advertising. We run no ads in the app.
  • No children's data: the app is a workplace tool for adult clinic staff. It is not directed at children, and children should not create accounts.

4. How long we keep it

  • Account details and inventory data: kept for as long as your account is active, and deleted when your account is deleted (see §5).
  • Photos/screenshots: kept with your facility's inventory data until you delete the item they belong to, or until your account is deleted, whichever comes first. Discarded imports (photos you reviewed and did not save) are deleted within 30 days.
  • Payment records: Stripe keeps payment records under its own policy. We keep the subscription record (plan, dates, last four digits) for as long as your account exists, and for up to 7 years after an account is deleted, only where tax or accounting law requires us to keep a record of a payment. We will say exactly what is retained and why in the deletion confirmation.
  • Support emails: kept for up to 2 years after the conversation ends, then deleted.
  • Crash/service records: kept for up to 90 days, then deleted.
  • Backups: deleted data may remain in encrypted backups for up to 30 days after deletion, after which it is overwritten and unrecoverable. Backups are never used to restore a deleted account.

5. How to delete your account and your data

You can delete your account at any time, in either of two ways, you do not need to reinstall the app to do it:

  1. In the app: go to Account settings → Delete account, and follow the prompts. Deleting your account deletes your account details and the inventory data that belongs to your account/facility, as described in the confirmation screen. (If you are the only administrator of a facility, deleting your account deletes that facility's data too, the app will warn you before you confirm.)
  2. On the web, without the app: go to https://expiryhealth.com/delete-account (or email info@expiryhealth.com from the email address on your account with the subject “Delete my ExpiryHealth account”). Tell us the email address on the account. We will confirm the request by email and then delete the account and its data.

We will complete a deletion request within 30 days of confirming it. Deactivation or freezing an account is not deletion, a deleted account and its data are gone, subject only to the payment-record and backup exceptions in §4. If you only want a copy of your data, or want a single item removed rather than the whole account, email info@expiryhealth.com and we will help.

You can also ask us at any time to correct your account details, or to tell you what data we hold about you, email info@expiryhealth.com from your account email address.

6. Security

  • All data travels over HTTPS (encrypted in transit) between the app, your browser, and our servers.
  • Passwords are stored only as secure hashes by our authentication provider; we never store a plain-text password.
  • Access to the database is limited to the systems that run the service; staff access, if ever needed to fix a problem, is logged.
  • No system is perfectly secure. If we ever discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you promptly by email and explain what happened and what we are doing about it.

ExpiryHealth is a workplace tool for adult clinic staff. It is not directed at children under 18, and children should not create accounts. If we learn that a child has created an account, we will delete it and the data with it.
